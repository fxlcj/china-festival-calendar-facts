# China Festival & Calendar Facts — verified dates with sources and check dates

A reference for the Chinese festival and calendar facts that guides most often get wrong: which public holidays fall when, how many statutory holidays there are, the exact minute each 2026 solar term begins, and the UNESCO listings behind Chinese calligraphy and tea.

Every row carries **the official Chinese (or UNESCO) source that states it** and **the date it was last checked**. If you copy a date anywhere, copy the check date with it — that is what makes it verifiable at all.

- `facts.json` — the same 36 entries, machine-readable

- The human-readable hub: <https://jiunotes.top/en/culture/>

- How entries are kept current: see [Maintenance](#maintenance)

## Why this exists

English-language guides to Chinese festivals are usually written once, from memory, and then age silently. Festival dates get quoted from the wrong year. Holiday arrangements get remembered from a year that no longer matches. Solar terms get described as fixed days when they are computed to the minute.

The dates themselves are not secret — China's State Council publishes the holiday arrangement each November, and the Purple Mountain Observatory publishes the astronomical calendar. The gap is that almost nobody ties the English sentence back to the Chinese source, or says when they last looked. This file exists to close that gap.

**Scope, and a note on honesty.** These are published dates, holiday arrangements, statutory holiday counts and official designations. They are not travel advice, they are not an official publication, and no holiday arrangement here is invented. Where a date could not be confirmed against an official page, it is not listed here — a shorter list beats a guessed date.

## What is *not* in this file

- **This year's exact festival dates beyond 2026/2027.** The next arrangement is only authoritative once the State Council publishes it (usually each November).

- **Subjective customs.** What people 'should' do or eat on a festival is opinion, not fact; only the date and the official holiday arrangement are listed.

- **Religious doctrine or sensitive topics.** These are excluded by design.

- **Anything we could not verify.** If no official page states it, it is not here.

## Maintenance

This dataset is not written once and abandoned:

- Every source URL is re-fetched on a monthly maintenance run, and the holiday arrangement is re-checked when the State Council publishes the next year's notice. When a page's content changes, the entry is flagged and the claim/figure is updated with a CHANGELOG line.

- Each entry carries a `checked_on` date in `facts.json`. If a figure changes, its date changes with it — a date is never quietly swapped while the old check date stays in place.
